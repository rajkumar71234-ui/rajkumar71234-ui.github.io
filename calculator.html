Before you touch a number

Money doubles. Money also shrinks.

The rule of 72

Divide 72 by the return and you have roughly the number of years it takes for the money to double. It is arithmetic you can do in your head, and it is close enough to be useful.

  • 6%12 years
  • 8%9 years
  • 10%7.2 years
  • 12%6 years
  • 14%5.1 years
  • 16%4.5 years
  • 18%4 years
  • 20%3.6 years
  • 22%3.3 years
  • 24%3 years
72 ÷ the annual return. Within a few months of the exact answer for anything between 6% and 20%. A higher number here is a promise nobody can make — it only tells you what a return would do, not what it will be.
What inflation takes

₹100 today, and what the same hundred rupees will actually buy later. Nothing leaves the account. It is what the money can carry home that falls.

₹100 divided by (1 + inflation) raised to the number of years. At 6% you need the money to double roughly every twelve years just to stand still — which is the first table again, read backwards.
